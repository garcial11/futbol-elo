How the ranking is calculated

These ratings use a pure Elo system, the same method used to rank chess players, applied to national teams. Nothing else is mixed in.

The idea

  • Every team starts at 1500.
  • After each match the winner takes points from the loser. Beating a strong team is worth more than beating a weak one; a bad loss costs more.
  • A draw moves points from the higher-rated team toward the lower-rated one.

The math

Before a match, each team's expected result is:

E = 1 / (1 + 10^((opponent − you) / 400))

After the match, the rating changes by:

new rating = rating + K × (actual − E)

actual = 1 for a win, 0.5 for a draw, 0 for a loss. K = sets how much a single match can move a rating.

What is deliberately ignored

Because it is "pure" Elo, and unlike FIFA's ranking or eloratings.net, it makes no adjustment for:

  • Goal margin — a 1–0 win counts exactly like a 5–0 win.
  • Match importance — a friendly moves ratings the same as a World Cup final.
  • Home advantage — the venue is not considered.

The data

Ratings are computed from every men's international on record ( matches, , teams), from the community-maintained martj42/international_results dataset, fetched live in your browser on each visit. Team names follow the dataset's FIFA-style lineage: West Germany is recorded as "Germany" and the Soviet Union as "Russia", while genuinely separate historical sides — East Germany ("German DR"), Czechoslovakia, and Yugoslavia — keep their own entries. Every rating is recomputed from scratch on each load, so corrections to old results appear automatically.
